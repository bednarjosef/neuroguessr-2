neuroguessr-2: image geolocation

Technical report · 24 July 2026 · single static image, no panorama, no metadata · all numbers on the frozen 2 998-image validation set
Where we are. A DINOv3 ViT-L/16 classifier over 5 000 learned geocells, followed by a retrieval stage that snaps the answer onto the nearest visually-matching image among 1.2 M geo-tagged exemplars. Median error 43.75 km, 42.9 % of images within 25 km, 11.3 % within 1 km, GeoGuessr equivalent 4 438 / 5 000 per round. For reference, PIGEON (Stanford, 2023) reports 131 km median and 24.2 % @25 km on single images.

1 · Task and evaluation

One 384×384 image in, one latitude/longitude out. Scoring is by great-circle (haversine) distance to the truth. The evaluator is frozen: the same 2 998 held-out images, the same metric code, never touched during research — so a score can be improved only by making the model better, never by making the test easier. Reported metrics:

  • median km — the primary objective (robust to the hopeless tail)
  • acc@R — share of images within R km, the fine-grained quality signal
  • mean km — dominated by the ~2.5 % of images sent to the wrong continent; it is effectively a country-accuracy metric, not a fine-grained one
  • GeoGuessr score — 5 000·e−d/1492.7, the game's world-map formula

Training: 3 epochs over 1 198 072 images (299 518 locations × 4 headings) on 4×RTX 5090, cross-entropy on distance-smoothed cell targets (softmax(−d/τ), τ = 75 km) plus an auxiliary mean-spherical-loss term that back-propagates through the differentiable spherical mean of the predicted distribution with a Geman–McClure robust penalty in kilometres.

2.2 Stage two — retrieval (where the accuracy actually comes from)

The classifier can never beat its own cell size: with 5 000 cells the quantisation floor is ~34 km median, and the argmax readout can do no better. Retrieval removes that floor entirely by returning the coordinates of a real training image rather than a cell centre.

query image │ ├─► encoder ──► descriptor (1024-d, L2-normalised) │ └─► classifier ──► posterior over 5 000 cells │ ▼ ① GATE: smallest set of cells holding 95 % of the posterior mass (median ~42 cells, capped at 400) │ ≈ 22 000 candidate images ▼ ② MATCH: cosine similarity in a learned "place" space score = 0.5·sim(head_A) + 0.5·sim(head_B) + 0.05·log p(cell) │ ▼ ③ RERANK: top-200 re-scored by regional chamfer similarity (4×4 grid of patch-block descriptors, PCA-whitened to 128-d) │ ▼ ④ SNAP: take the single best match, output ITS coordinates

The index holds 1 198 072 descriptors (2.4 GB fp16 per space) plus 1.2 M × 16 × 128 regional descriptors (4.9 GB). One guess costs ~1.2 s on a laptop CPU.

2.3 The place heads — the decisive idea

Raw DINOv3 similarity answers "do these two photos look alike", which is the wrong question: two photos of the same 30 m of forest road taken 180° apart look nothing alike, while a Finnish road and a Canadian road look identical. Each head is a small residual MLP (1024→2048→1024, output layer zero-initialised so it starts as the identity) trained contrastively on cached descriptors — minutes of GPU time, no images touched.

The correction that mattered: the first head used same-location positives. But at test time the query's own location is never in the index — the best reachable answer is a different location 1–15 km away, and same-location training actively pushes those neighbours apart. Retraining with positives drawn from any location within 10 km, and masking in-batch negatives closer than 50 km as false negatives, moved the median from 61.2 → 49.0 km.

5 · Where the remaining error lives

Three ceilings, all measured on the same val set:

  • Coverage: 100 %. For every single validation image there exists a training image within 25 km (median nearest 2.2 km). Data is not the constraint.
  • In-pool oracle: 92–98 %. Even restricted to the candidates the gate actually shows, a perfect matcher would score 92–98 % @25 km.
  • We convert that into 42.9 %.

So ~50 points of @25 km sit purely in matching quality — not in coverage, not in the gate, not in the cell scheme. Two rounds of head engineering moved it 38.7 → 42.9 with sharply diminishing returns, which is the evidence that the limit is in the features themselves: a backbone tuned to classify continents keeps what distinguishes Finland from Canada and discards what distinguishes one Finnish road from the next.

7 · In flight

C3 — contrastive fine-tune of the encoder itself. Every head so far could only re-weight information the frozen backbone already kept. C3 trains the backbone (LoRA) so that cosine similarity is geographic proximity: positives within 10 km, negatives drawn from the same ~500 km region so they are visually similar but geographically wrong, near-pairs masked out of the loss, embeddings gathered across all four GPUs for a large negative pool. The classifier is deliberately untouched — the retrieval gate keeps using the old checkpoint's logits, so a worse encoder cannot poison the gate. Expected @25 km: 46–49 %.
